About

Bridging observations, simulations, and machine learning

Galaxies are not points. The processes that build and quench them, including gas inflow, compaction, stellar and black-hole feedback, mergers, and environment, act in specific places within galaxies and on specific timescales.

I am an extragalactic astrophysicist working at the intersection of astrophysics and data science. I map the spatially resolved physical properties of galaxies with imaging and spectroscopic data from various telescopes (including HST, JWST, and Euclid), and compare them with cosmological hydrodynamical simulations. I build the open-source tools that link the two: piXedfit for spatially resolved SED fitting, together with its successor piXedfit2 (in development), which combines traditional Bayesian SED fitting and machine-learning inference in one modular, interactive framework; GalSyn for forward-modeling simulations into realistic observations; and GalSyn-SBI, a set of simulation-based inference (SBI) networks that recover global and resolved galaxy properties in under a second. These tools are built for the coming flood of data from Euclid, Roman, and Rubin/LSST.

I was PI of JWST Cycle 2 program GO 4246 on the study of a merging galaxy at the cosmic dawn epoch, and I am an active member of the Euclid Consortium and the Roman Science Collaboration, and a co-investigator on more than a dozen JWST programs. Before Indiana, I was a postdoctoral fellow at Johns Hopkins University and ASIAA (Taiwan), and I earned my PhD at Tohoku University (Japan).

Research

From pixels to physics

One question drives my program: how do galaxies assemble their internal structures and shut down their star formation across 12 billion years, and which physical processes set the routes they take? I answer it with a closed loop between simulations and survey data.

Full research program →
  1. Simulations provide labeled ground truth: galaxies whose full history is known.
  2. GalSyn forward-models them into realistic synthetic data (HST, JWST, Euclid, Roman, and Rubin).
  3. SBI networks (GalSyn-SBI) trained on these mocks infer global and resolved properties in <1 s per galaxy.
  4. Applied to real surveys, they yield resolved maps and SFHs for 105–107 galaxies.
  5. These maps test hypotheses about quenching routes and structural assembly.
  6. The results constrain the physics in the next generation of simulations.

piXedfit

Pixelized SED fitting · ApJS 2021

An end-to-end package for spatially resolved SED fitting: image processing, PSF matching, imaging–IFS combination, pixel binning, and Bayesian SED fitting.

piXedfit2

Next-generation piXedfit · in development

A modular, pythonic rewrite of piXedfit for interactive resolved SED analysis from survey mosaics, with five inference backends: RDSPS, MCMC, Dense Basis, Bagpipes, and SBI.

GalSyn-SBI

Resolved galaxy inference · in development

Simulation-based inference that turns multiband images into calibrated maps of stellar mass, SFR, age, metallicity, and dust in under a second, using a U-Net and normalizing flows trained on GalSyn mocks.
