Research program

Resolving galaxies across cosmic time

JWST now resolves galaxies at kiloparsec scales out to z ~ 8, and Euclid, Rubin/LSST, and Roman will image billions more. The bottleneck has moved from data to inference. My program connects simulations and observations through forward modeling and machine learning, so that differences between data and models reflect physics, not method.

Methodology

The “Pixels to Physics” loop

Cosmological simulations (1) are forward-modeled with GalSyn (2) into realistic multiband observations with known ground truth. These mocks train amortized simulation-based inference networks (3). Applied to JWST, Euclid, Rubin, and Roman imaging, the networks produce resolved maps and star-formation histories for 105–107 galaxies (4). These test physical hypotheses (5) and feed constraints back to the next generation of simulations (6).

Mock and real galaxies pass through the same pipeline, so inference biases can be measured where the answer is known.

Future directions

Looking ahead

Four connected thrusts. Each pairs a falsifiable science goal with a data-science innovation the survey era needs.

THRUST1

When, where, and how do galaxies shut down?

Is the map of quenching routes across mass and cosmic time a single pattern that scales with epoch, or did the early Universe quench galaxies through routes that are absent later?

SBI applied to all public JWST/NIRCam imaging in five deep fields: ~3×105 galaxies at 1.5 < z < 8. Training on GalSyn mocks from TNG, EAGLE+FLARES, SIMBA, and THESAN tests four hypotheses on outshining biases, the origin of the first massive quiescent galaxies, transition masses, and temporary low-mass quenching.

JWSTquenching routesoutshining
THRUST2

From JWST to the survey era: a resolved census with Euclid, Roman, and Rubin/LSST

How do environment and the cosmic web shape where star formation dies inside galaxies, and how do bulges, disks, and faint outskirts assemble?

The JWST archive serves as an observed truth set. Degrading it to Euclid and Roman resolution calibrates survey-trained networks before they are applied to 106–107 galaxies. The data-science work covers sim-to-real domain shift, ML deblending, photo-z marginalization, and spatially varying PSFs.

EuclidRoman · R-HIVEdomain adaptation
THRUST3

Using resolved galaxies to constrain galaxy-formation physics

Can resolved galaxy populations discriminate between, and quantitatively constrain, the sub-grid physics in simulations?

The plan has three parts: distribution-level comparison of kinetic, thermal, and jet AGN feedback models; SBI over CAMELS feedback parameters using resolved summary statistics; and a JAX-accelerated, differentiable dust module in GalSyn that constrains attenuation curves at high redshift. All of this requires robust inference under model misspecification.

CAMELSdifferentiable modelsmisspecification
THRUST4

Discovery at small scales and rare phases

Do giant star-forming clumps survive, migrate inward, and build bulges?

A statistical sample of lensed clumps and star clusters from the VENUS and Cosmic Spring collaborations, compared with zoom simulations forward-modeled with GalSyn. ML anomaly detection in SBI latent space will look for short-lived phases such as compaction caught in the act, rejuvenation, and the hosts of compact red AGN.
